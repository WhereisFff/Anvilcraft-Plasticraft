# Universal Plastic

A universal-plastic-melt fluid block placed in the world always solidifies in place as one universal plastic block, regardless of which world mechanism caused the change. Rain, adjacent water, and blocks in `anvilcraftplasticraft:plastic_melt_coolants` are current examples. The product keeps the melt's colour and the world conversion never also drops granules.

The block is `16 px` wide, `16 px` long, and `14 px` high. It has no menu, inventory, tank, fluid capability, or special storage. It retains the common plastic-product behaviour instead: gravity, buoyancy, dynamic attraction, pushing, sliding-rail transport, magnetisation, adhesive bonding, persistence, clicked-face placement, hammer quick-use and six-face rotation, and colour-preserving recovery. When placing it, the clicked face is split into four regions by its diagonals; the edge nearest the targeted region selects the in-plane direction, exposing four orientations on each of the six faces. While a product is held, thick white lines mark the targeted face and both diagonals, and the full face uses alternating translucent-white and transparent grid cells. The active region's three edges and white cells turn green. The green selection slides smoothly when its region changes, while the complete preview slides into place when another block face is targeted. Hammer rotation uses the centre of the actual outline bounds, so flipping between upright and upside-down does not shift the block because of its missing `2 px`; a target orientation that would overlap a block or entity renders pale red and is not applied on release. Its texture is generated from the common plastic bases and the selected 16-level colour palette. The generator selects a base by the longest side of each exposed face: sides up to `8 px`, `16 px`, and `32 px` use `8x8`, `16x16`, and `32x32` respectively, while every side over `32 px` uses `48x48`. Base greys use their absolute brightness across `0..255` as the mapping baseline, so a base that uses only a bright range is not forcibly darkened. Adjacent greys that quantise to the same palette level are separated in their original brightness order, preventing corner transitions from merging into a double-width dark patch. Shading drawn into each edge and corner is retained without a uniform edge highlight replacing it. The four sides match and the pattern is centre-symmetric, so it does not lighten the top-left and darken the bottom-right. Brightness steps blend with pixel dithering instead of hard edges; the inset groove stays readable, and large faces stay mostly clean. Resource reloads regenerate it from all four complete edited base patterns, preserving the border around every exposed face.

The Plasticraft creative tab shows one white Universal Plastic Melt Bucket, Universal Plastic Granule, and full `16x16x16 px` Universal Plastic Block. Left-clicking these entries keeps the vanilla white-item pickup behaviour; right-clicking opens the `78x80 px` sixteen-colour overlay regardless of Shift or other modifiers, and right-clicking the source entry again closes it. The overlay shares its centre axis with the source slot, and its bottom edge touches the slot's top edge. Its `4x4` cells run left to right and top to bottom as white, light gray, gray, black, brown, red, orange, yellow, lime, green, cyan, light blue, blue, purple, magenta, and pink. Left- or right-clicking a cell applies the current vanilla modifier semantics, including Shift, to that colour. While a cell is hovered, Q drops one item and Ctrl+Q drops a maximum-sized stack using the normal creative action. A click outside the texture closes the overlay before the same click continues, while covered pixels block the slots beneath; a partly covered slot therefore remains usable through its exposed part. The stack carried by the cursor renders above the overlay. E, Esc, and every other inventory-closing action close the overlay with the inventory.

For gravity-affected blocks such as sand and anvils, the `14 px` top is not a block-grid-aligned landing surface: a block that is already falling shatters into its corresponding drop on impact. A gravity-affected block placed directly one cell above universal plastic remains a block until the logical cell below is completely unoccupied by the plastic entity.

## Chest, tank, anvil, and tray products

The molding chamber offers normal products, chests, tanks, anvils, trays, and propellers in its designated-type list. Chests and tanks require a fully sealed six-neighbour shell, an empty cavity, and `C/(C+S) >= 0.5`. Zero-thickness faces may seal a boundary without adding to `S`. Multiple sealed cavities form one logical capacity, exterior decoration is outside the denominator, and suspended or cavity-intruding elements fail the empty-cavity check.

A chest derives `floor(C / 64)` item slots and needs at least `64` cubic pixels, with no extra upper limit. `1728` cavity pixels produce `27` slots; `63` fails and `64` produces `1` slot. Chests have no menu and expose an item capability to hoppers, chutes, and other automation. Sparse slot contents survive item recovery, world placement, and bonded block state; Jade and the Anvil Hammer HUD send and draw only a bounded item summary.

A tank derives `floor(C / 171)` B and needs at least `171` cubic pixels, with no extra upper limit. `2744` cavity pixels produce `16 B`; `170` fails and `171` produces `1 B`. One shared capacity stores multiple component-aware `FluidStack` values in stable order. Automation can insert or extract by fluid and components, drain in saved order, and hand-held fluid containers use the same capability. The visible liquid uses an equal-volume half-space cut of the cavity along the current effective gravity direction; continuously changing gravity is interpolated before the plane is solved, so multiple cavities and fluids preserve volume and order.

The anvil type scans the model on its world Y axis: the lowest occupied layer is the bottom, and an arbitrary rotated direction is never treated as the bottom. That layer must contain a continuous, hole-free flat rectangle of at least `14x14 px`. A reversed cube on the same lowest horizontal plane may instead supply a negative outer outline: each outline edge contributes its `1 px` boundary, so the imported Ember Anvil's `12x12 px` occupied footprint qualifies as a `14x14 px` negative bottom while an ordinary `12x12 px` bottom does not. The model must be at least `10 px` tall and split along Y into bottom, middle, and top sections at least `3/3/4 px` thick; the top must be strictly thicker than the bottom. Adjacent layers must overlap so all three sections connect. The middle projection must be fully inside the bottom projection with all four bounds strictly inset. The top projection is compared by total pixel area; any individual bound may match or sit inside the bottom, and its total area may be up to and including `16 px²` smaller than the bottom. A valid product is named "Universal Plastic Anvil" and has the same falling-anvil recipe capability and anvil GUI as a hardened resin anvil.

Only an impact with the model's local bottom face pointing down in the world publishes AnvilCraft's `AnvilEvent.OnLand`; side, top, and upside-down impacts do not start falling-anvil processing. The default anvil predicate can match it, while predicates with dedicated properties, NBT, or other anvil-only restrictions are not broadened. Right-clicking the product or using the anvil hammer opens the vanilla anvil menu. Universal Plastic uses separate `176x166` backgrounds, `universal_plastic_anvil_base.png` and `universal_plastic_anvil_overlay.png`: the first is a gray-white checker placeholder tinted by the plastic colour, and the second is an untinted purple-black checker placeholder. Future plastic materials keep the same base/overlay pair.

If the lowest layer contains a complete flat `40x40 px` rectangle, including exactly `40x40 px`, the product also has Giant Anvil abilities. Its bottom-face impact can run Giant Anvil multiblock crafting and conversion, trigger ground shocks over a Heavy Iron Block base, and process a Large Cauldron. A `39x40 px` bottom remains a valid ordinary plastic anvil but does not gain these abilities.

A tray is always scanned on the model's world Y axis; no rotated direction can become its bottom. Every element in a normal tray model must remain in the `48x48 px` modeling area at `x/z=[0,48)`, which defines nine fixed `16x16 px` mounting cells, and the complete height may not exceed `4 px`. Volumetric cubes must provide completely horizontal bottom and top faces at the model's true minimum and maximum Y, so a Y-axis rotation that keeps both faces level is valid but an X/Z-axis slope is not. The two footprints may differ, be disconnected, contain holes, or omit the exact model center. Bottom thickness belongs to the bottom itself, and no continuity or filled-area rule applies to the remaining shape between the two faces. A cell becomes mountable when any part of it is occupied by a volumetric Cube; zero-thickness Cubes do not provide support. Oversized creative-generator products still use only these fixed nine cells, so geometry outside the area neither adds mounting cells nor raises the nine-component limit.

Right-click a supported empty cell while holding a supported component to mount one component in that cell, up to one per cell and nine per tray; sneak-right-click an existing component with an empty hand to recover the component from that exact cell. Supported components are vanilla redstone repeaters, comparators, redstone torches, all buttons, levers, all pressure plates including AnvilCraft variants, and daylight detectors, plus AnvilCraft advanced comparators, pulse generators, and item detectors. Every component's selection outline joins the tray's interaction outline and entity bounds, so each exposed model can be targeted directly; any physical collision shape supplied by a component also joins the tray's collision geometry in both dynamic and bonded states. The original block models are rendered on their respective plastic cells, and each button, lever, pressure plate, daylight detector, repeater, comparator, advanced comparator, pulse generator, or item detector keeps independent runtime state, interactions, and redstone behaviour, including relevant menus and Structure Disk configuration. Rendering and world light both use the component's native light emission for its current block state. Among the currently supported components, a lit vanilla redstone torch emits block light level `7`; states whose native emission is `0` do not gain extra world light. Extinguishing, moving, rotating, removing, or recovering a component, removing its entity, and handing it between dynamic and bonded states all refresh the light source.

Horizontally adjacent cells exchange directional signals inside the tray before consulting world input on that face, so a button directly drives an adjacent repeater whose receiving end faces the button; repeater side input, priority, and delay retain their existing behaviour. A shared face between two occupied cells is reserved for this internal connection and does not also project output into the world. Every other output face maps from the component's actual cell to the corresponding world position. Tray input samples whether an adjacent signal can activate the mounted component, so a source that only supplies weak activation can still drive a mounted repeater. This is separate from the full-sized plastic conductor's direct-power-only rule and does not depend on recognizing vanilla dust or AnvilCraft wire by block type. A moving or off-grid tray maps each input port from the cells covered by that component's real face. Inputs inspect the layer touching that face first and take the strongest signal within it, consulting the layer one cell farther out only when the near layer has no signal. Each output range uses the plastic entity's placement-contact face as its base: it is that mounting cell translated one cell in the component's output direction and extended one cell away from the contact face, and it continuously follows the entity's position and orientation. Air and fluid-only blocks do not count as receivers; when the range intersects multiple world cells, output projects only to the actual block nearest the output endpoint. If that block can be strongly powered, it still activates adjacent blocks according to vanilla rules; this is normal propagation by the receiver, not direct tray output beyond the range. Local front, back, left, right, top, and bottom map onto all six world directions across all 24 product orientations, and a bonded tray preserves every cell, component state, and port. The tray type icon uses a bundled white `16x16x2 px` open-frame tray model through the normal procedural plastic item renderer. The bundled data never reads the save's blueprint directory, so deleting the original `untitled-3` blueprint does not affect the icon.

Chest and tank base tooltips show their total capacity and do not change with current contents; the ordinary-anvil tooltip describes its falling-anvil capability, the giant-anvil tooltip also lists multiblock processing, ground shocks, and Large Cauldron processing, and the tray tooltip states that supported modeling cells can carry up to nine supported redstone components. Chest and tank items also show a separate contents tooltip directly above the pointer, outside the base tooltip. It uses the vanilla tooltip frame rather than a dedicated texture. The chest's "Occupied slots" line and the tank's "Stored fluid" line are white; the remaining per-content lines are light gray, and every line keeps the default dark-gray shadow. Current chest items also appear in Jade and the Anvil Hammer HUD. A tank uses Jade's standard fluid bar, while the Anvil Hammer HUD shows its total and per-fluid contents; its per-item and per-fluid lines use the same light-gray text and dark-gray shadow. If partial forming no longer satisfies the selected chest, tank, anvil, or tray validator, the shape is retained and the product is downgraded to a normal product.

## Molded products

Normal products made by a Plastic Molding Chamber use the same `anvilcraftplasticraft:universal_plastic` item ID, entity ID, and `UniversalPlasticEntity` class as the `16x16x14 px` block cooled directly from melt; they are not a second entity kind. Their item data stores the actual formed shape, model hash, melt material and colour, and stable pivot. Importing and forming a `16x16x14 px` cuboid therefore gives the same collision shape, interaction outline, rotation centre, and common plastic behaviour as direct cooling in all 24 orientations. Every source cube retains its original endpoints and rotation, so continuous slopes are not refitted into steps from the `1 px` manufacturing cells. When melt is short, the whole model is cut horizontally at the paid forming percentage: the cut has a flat top while every remaining sloped side keeps its original angle. The inventory model, world entity, bonded block state, Jade icon, hammer orientation wheel, and adhesive endpoint preview all derive from the same synchronized surface data and share one cached result from Plasticraft's public procedural-texture API. Valid hammer and adhesive endpoint previews use the standard pale-blue plastic overlay; rejected previews use pale red. The world-aligned direction axes are centred on the model's actual rotation pivot. Inventory rendering normalises the longest visible surface-bounds axis to the same footprint as an ordinary universal plastic block and centres the actual surface; this does not change world size. A world entity samples block and sky light at the centre and six outer faces of its real bounds, so a small or offset model does not turn black on an opaque floor because its declared origin lies elsewhere. Hammer recovery, chunk reloads, and cross-dimensional travel do not reduce the product to the legacy block shape, and holding the item adds no placement preview.

A molded item's and entity's display name does not use the blueprint or model name. It combines the plastic material name with the final functional-type suffix: universal-plastic normal, chest, tank, anvil, and tray products are named "Universal Plastic Block", "Universal Plastic Chest", "Universal Plastic Tank", "Universal Plastic Anvil", and "Universal Plastic Tray". Future materials and functional types follow the same composition rule.

A molded entity has 24 discrete orientations: six attachment faces, each with four quarter-turns in its plane. Entity persistence and network synchronisation preserve both parts, while the saved manufacturing pivot keeps rendering, interaction outline, and physical collision aligned. Item placement follows the in-plane four-region rule above. Survival-mode breaking, hammer recovery, or pick-blocking an entity in any rotation returns the same model and material in the default item orientation; a Creative-mode attack removes the entity without dropping an item. A damaging attack that destroys any movable plastic entity plays the vanilla block-break particles and sound for its synchronized display state at the centre of its real geometry.

Each complete or horizontally cut volumetric source cube becomes one continuous convex collision body. A dynamic entity uses those bodies directly, so a sloped cube keeps sloped physics and sloped F3+B edges instead of becoming many small boxes. A bonded block registers that same set of real convex bodies as SAT obstacles, preserving slopes for the final movement clipping of players, mobs, dropped items, and plastic entities. It also exposes a coarse `VoxelShape` made from one bounding box per body, but that shape is only for vanilla block APIs, broad-phase enumeration, and selection candidates; it neither overrides the real collision nor returns to per-pixel fitting. Bonded blocks cache interaction and collision `VoxelShape` instances separately and invalidate them only when the synchronized model, orientation, or display snapshot changes, so standing on a complex blockified model does not rebuild all coarse geometry for every collision query. A zero-thickness cube renders on both sides and has a very thin interaction-only outline, so it can be selected and recovered but cannot support, block, or push an entity. Shapes up to the full 3x3x3 workspace retain the common plastic physics for gravity, buoyancy, dynamic attraction, pushing, sliding rails, magnetisation, adhesive bonding, persistence, and recovery.

While a piston builds its push list, every cell covered by a dynamic plastic entity's real collision shape presents its blockified state only for that structure resolution, and all cells of the same entity consume one push position together. A piston facing a plastic entity of any size therefore continues into and pushes every block obstructed in front of its occupied cells, while an immovable block in any such path correctly prevents extension. These read-only placeholders are removed from the block movement list before execution, so they never create moving-piston blocks, settle as blocks, or duplicate the entity. A sticky piston normally retracts a plastic entity touching its head; a short pulse of no more than `2 gt` instead preserves vanilla block-spitting behavior and pushes the plastic entity out without pulling it back. A neighboring plastic entity joined to a slime block, honey block, or High-Viscosity Resin Block in the push structure also moves one block with that structure during both extension and retraction. During that motion its collision stays aligned with the sticky block, so the front top face does not leave a standable gap.

Entity movement, short support probes, stepping, carrying, and side pushing share these bodies and the same continuous SAT contact. Players, mobs, dropped items, and other supported entities follow one rule: movement over a slope stays on its real surface, and sneaking stops only at its real support edge. A plastic product supported by another entity receives its carried displacement during that supporter's current move instead of catching up on a later entity tick. Movement normal to and away from the product does not drag it along, but a gravity component in the same request does not cancel tangential carrying. Pushing a rotated cube transfers only the component aimed along the real contact normal, while the pusher retains movement along the face. A sloped product on a player's head uses those real convex bodies for pose checks instead of the compatibility bounding box, so it does not force sneaking or crawling. If a product carried on the head is blocked by a narrow gap, the player can keep walking out from under it; the product stays behind and falls once it loses head support. A blocked side-push chain still clips the pusher. Server-side player movement validation uses the same real convex bodies, so it does not mistake a compatibility bounding-box edge for a surface that can be stood on. When a block collision surface is within `1 px` ahead of a side push, the product completes that last short distance and immediately sits against the surface; it does not snap across a gap larger than `1 px`. An observer emits its vanilla pulse after `2 gt` only when its observing face gains or loses contact with a real plastic convex body; stationary contact neither retriggers it nor causes a world scan every tick. Loading a save or reloading a chunk restores an existing face contact without emitting another pulse.

When a molded product's visible surface bounds are exactly `16 px` on X, Y, and Z, it receives and conducts redstone through all six faces with vanilla solid-block strong/weak-power semantics. Each face extends a port range exactly one block outward from the real surface. A grid-aligned range covers only the touching layer; an off-grid range can intersect two adjacent layers, in which case the nearest layer containing a signal wins and its strongest value is used. Input accepts only direct strong power aimed into that face, with no block-type exceptions, so weak activation that can only trigger an adjacent component does not pass through the full-sized plastic. Dust can strongly power the plastic and thereby activate an ordinary component on the opposite face, but while downstream dust calculates its input, vanilla temporarily suppresses dust output; this prevents a `dust -> plastic -> dust` bridge. Output follows the entity and, within the same one-block range, chooses the nearest layer and then the non-air, non-fluid-only receiver closest to the port centre. Grid-aligned input and output therefore cannot cross a complete empty cell, while an off-grid port can select only cells physically intersected by its one-block range. The plastic supplies weak output rather than direct strong power and ignores virtual output from another full-sized plastic conductor, so it neither propagates through a second solid block nor latches after the external source is removed. The ordinary `16x16x14 px` universal plastic and every molded product with any axis other than `16 px` do not conduct.

An unspecified product, an explicitly normal product, and any future product downgraded to normal after partial forming all use the tooltip "An ordinary piece of plastic". Every universal plastic item also shows its exact three-axis size in blocks with at most four necessary decimal places. The ordinary `16x16x14 px` block displays `Size: 1 x 0.875 x 1 blocks`; a molded product uses its exact visible surface bounds.

## Carry synchronisation and collision outlines

When a plastic product is genuinely supported by an entity's head or another face, it receives the tangential displacement during that carrier's current move. The client keeps ordered, unconfirmed movement segments per X, Y, and Z axis, so an immediate reversal does not cancel the two steps before the server confirms them. Snapshots consume the path in order, and a temporarily stationary snapshot does not discard that pending path; an upside-down hardened-resin cauldron therefore stays on a contacting rim or inner ceiling in the same tick and cannot be interpolated into the player's pose. A sloped face on the head likewise keeps the standing pose. If the carried product is wider than a gap, the player can walk into the opening; the product stops when blocked and falls after it loses head support. Support, side pushing, and ordinary entities still use the same continuous convex bodies, while empty space inside the cauldron is not support.

With F3+B enabled, convex plastic collision is drawn as the real edge set of the union of every source cube. Shared faces, coplanar seams, and collinear segments are merged; concave openings, overlap boundaries, rotated cubes, and continuous slopes remain visible. The debug outline never falls back to a compatibility `VoxelShape` or one total bounding box; that compatibility shape remains limited to vanilla APIs and broad-phase queries. When the crosshair points at dynamic or bonded plastic, its selection outline reuses the same real edge set in black with an alpha of `0.4`; a dynamic outline follows the current interpolated render position and does not add a total bounding box.

## Plastic granules

Granules are deliberately limited to recipe or container processing. Each of these routes produces 16 granules with the melt's colour per `1000 mB`:

- the existing solid-liquid cooling recipe with a cold item;
- a Catalytic Press Lid outlet aimed at a full vanilla water cauldron.

These routes do not change the rule for a melt fluid block in the world. Each novice AnvilCraft Jeweler offer randomly requests one of the 16 colours, buying 8 granules of that colour for 2 emeralds, up to 16 trades per offer.
