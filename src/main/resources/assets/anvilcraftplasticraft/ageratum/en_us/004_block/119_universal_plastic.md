# Universal Plastic

A universal-plastic-melt fluid block placed in the world always solidifies in place as one universal plastic block, regardless of which world mechanism caused the change. Rain, adjacent water, and blocks in `anvilcraftplasticraft:plastic_melt_coolants` are current examples. The product keeps the melt's colour and the world conversion never also drops granules.

The block is `16 px` wide, `16 px` long, and `14 px` high. It has no menu, inventory, tank, fluid capability, or special storage. It retains the common plastic-product behaviour instead: gravity, buoyancy, dynamic attraction, pushing, sliding-rail transport, magnetisation, adhesive bonding, persistence, clicked-face placement, hammer quick-use and six-face rotation, and colour-preserving recovery. When placing it, the clicked face is split into four regions by its diagonals; the edge nearest the targeted region selects the in-plane direction, exposing four orientations on each of the six faces. While a product is held, thick white lines mark the targeted face and both diagonals, and the full face uses alternating translucent-white and transparent grid cells. The active region's three edges and white cells turn green. The green selection slides smoothly when its region changes, while the complete preview slides into place when another block face is targeted. Hammer rotation uses the centre of the actual outline bounds, so flipping between upright and upside-down does not shift the block because of its missing `2 px`; a target orientation that would overlap a block or entity renders pale red and is not applied on release. Its texture is generated from the common plastic base and the selected 16-level colour palette. Base greys map by absolute brightness across `0..255`, so a base that uses only a bright range is not forcibly darkened. Resource reloads regenerate it from the complete edited base pattern, preserving the border around every exposed face.

For gravity-affected blocks such as sand and anvils, the `14 px` top is not a block-grid-aligned landing surface: a block that is already falling shatters into its corresponding drop on impact. A gravity-affected block placed directly one cell above universal plastic remains a block until the logical cell below is completely unoccupied by the plastic entity.

## Chest, tank, anvil, and tray products

The molding chamber offers normal products, chests, tanks, anvils, trays, and propellers in its designated-type list. Chests and tanks require a fully sealed six-neighbour shell, an empty cavity, and `C/(C+S) >= 0.5`. Zero-thickness faces may seal a boundary without adding to `S`. Multiple sealed cavities form one logical capacity, exterior decoration is outside the denominator, and suspended or cavity-intruding elements fail the empty-cavity check.

A chest derives `floor(C / 64)` item slots and needs at least `64` cubic pixels, with no extra upper limit. `1728` cavity pixels produce `27` slots; `63` fails and `64` produces `1` slot. Chests have no menu and expose an item capability to hoppers, chutes, and other automation. Sparse slot contents survive item recovery, world placement, and bonded block state; Jade and the Anvil Hammer HUD send and draw only a bounded item summary.

A tank derives `floor(C / 171)` B and needs at least `171` cubic pixels, with no extra upper limit. `2744` cavity pixels produce `16 B`; `170` fails and `171` produces `1 B`. One shared capacity stores multiple component-aware `FluidStack` values in stable order. Automation can insert or extract by fluid and components, drain in saved order, and hand-held fluid containers use the same capability. The visible liquid uses an equal-volume half-space cut of the cavity along the current effective gravity direction; continuously changing gravity is interpolated before the plane is solved, so multiple cavities and fluids preserve volume and order.

The anvil type scans the model on its world Y axis: the lowest occupied layer is the bottom, and an arbitrary rotated direction is never treated as the bottom. That layer must contain a continuous, hole-free flat rectangle of at least `14x14 px`. A reversed cube on the same lowest horizontal plane may instead supply a negative outer outline: each outline edge contributes its `1 px` boundary, so the imported Ember Anvil's `12x12 px` occupied footprint qualifies as a `14x14 px` negative bottom while an ordinary `12x12 px` bottom does not. The model must be at least `10 px` tall and split along Y into bottom, middle, and top sections at least `3/3/4 px` thick; the top must be strictly thicker than the bottom. Adjacent layers must overlap so all three sections connect. The middle projection must be fully inside the bottom projection with all four bounds strictly inset. The top projection is compared by total pixel area; any individual bound may match or sit inside the bottom, and its total area may be up to and including `16 px²` smaller than the bottom. A valid product is named "Universal Plastic Anvil" and has the same falling-anvil recipe capability and anvil GUI as a hardened resin anvil.

Only an impact with the model's local bottom face pointing down in the world publishes AnvilCraft's `AnvilEvent.OnLand`; side, top, and upside-down impacts do not start falling-anvil processing. The default anvil predicate can match it, while predicates with dedicated properties, NBT, or other anvil-only restrictions are not broadened. Right-clicking the product or using the anvil hammer opens the vanilla anvil menu. Universal Plastic uses separate `176x166` backgrounds, `universal_plastic_anvil_base.png` and `universal_plastic_anvil_overlay.png`: the first is a gray-white checker placeholder tinted by the plastic colour, and the second is an untinted purple-black checker placeholder. Future plastic materials keep the same base/overlay pair.

If the lowest layer contains a complete flat `40x40 px` rectangle, including exactly `40x40 px`, the product also has Giant Anvil abilities. Its bottom-face impact can run Giant Anvil multiblock crafting and conversion, trigger ground shocks over a Heavy Iron Block base, and process a Large Cauldron. A `39x40 px` bottom remains a valid ordinary plastic anvil but does not gain these abilities.

A tray is always scanned on the model's world Y axis; no rotated direction can become its bottom. Every element must remain in the central modeling cell at `x/z=[16,32)`, and the complete height may not exceed `4 px`. Volumetric cubes must provide completely horizontal bottom and top faces at the model's true minimum and maximum Y, so a Y-axis rotation that keeps both faces level is valid but an X/Z-axis slope is not. The two footprints may differ, be disconnected, or contain holes; each only needs to cover the exact `x=24, z=24` center point. Bottom thickness belongs to the bottom itself, and no continuity or filled-area rule applies to the remaining shape between the two faces.

Right-click an empty tray while holding a supported component to mount it; sneak-right-click with an empty hand to recover it. Supported components are vanilla redstone repeaters, comparators, redstone torches, all buttons, levers, all pressure plates including AnvilCraft variants, and daylight detectors, plus AnvilCraft advanced comparators, pulse generators, and item detectors. The component's selection outline joins the tray's interaction outline and entity bounds, so its exposed model can be targeted directly; any physical collision shape supplied by the component also joins the tray's collision geometry in both dynamic and bonded states. The original block model is rendered on the plastic surface, and button, lever, pressure-plate, daylight-detector, repeater, comparator, advanced-comparator, pulse-generator, and item-detector interactions and redstone behaviour remain available, including relevant menus and Structure Disk configuration. A moving or off-grid tray maps input ports from the cells covered by the component's real face. Inputs inspect the layer touching that face first and take the strongest signal within it, consulting the layer one cell farther out only when the near layer has no signal. The output range uses the plastic entity's placement-contact face as its base: it is the central cell translated one cell in the component's output direction and extended one cell away from the contact face, and it continuously follows the entity's position and orientation. Air and fluid-only blocks do not count as receivers; when the range intersects multiple world cells, output projects only to the actual block nearest the output endpoint. If that block can be strongly powered, it still activates adjacent blocks according to vanilla rules; this is normal propagation by the receiver, not direct tray output beyond the range. Its local front, back, left, right, top, and bottom map onto all six world directions across all 24 product orientations, and a bonded tray uses the same ports and runtime state. The tray type icon uses a bundled white `16x16x2 px` open-frame tray model through the normal procedural plastic item renderer. The bundled data never reads the save's blueprint directory, so deleting the original `untitled-3` blueprint does not affect the icon.

Chest and tank base tooltips show their total capacity and do not change with current contents; the ordinary-anvil tooltip describes its falling-anvil capability, the giant-anvil tooltip also lists multiblock processing, ground shocks, and Large Cauldron processing, and the tray tooltip states only that it can carry some redstone components. Chest and tank items also show a separate contents tooltip directly above the pointer, outside the base tooltip. It uses the vanilla tooltip frame rather than a dedicated texture. The chest's "Occupied slots" line and the tank's "Stored fluid" line are white; the remaining per-content lines are light gray, and every line keeps the default dark-gray shadow. Current chest items also appear in Jade and the Anvil Hammer HUD. A tank uses Jade's standard fluid bar, while the Anvil Hammer HUD shows its total and per-fluid contents; its per-item and per-fluid lines use the same light-gray text and dark-gray shadow. If partial forming no longer satisfies the selected chest, tank, anvil, or tray validator, the shape is retained and the product is downgraded to a normal product.

## Molded products

Normal products made by a Plastic Molding Chamber use the same `anvilcraftplasticraft:universal_plastic` item ID, entity ID, and `UniversalPlasticEntity` class as the `16x16x14 px` block cooled directly from melt; they are not a second entity kind. Their item data stores the actual formed shape, model hash, melt material and colour, and stable pivot. Importing and forming a `16x16x14 px` cuboid therefore gives the same collision shape, interaction outline, rotation centre, and common plastic behaviour as direct cooling in all 24 orientations. Every source cube retains its original endpoints and rotation, so continuous slopes are not refitted into steps from the `1 px` manufacturing cells. When melt is short, the whole model is cut horizontally at the paid forming percentage: the cut has a flat top while every remaining sloped side keeps its original angle. The inventory model, world entity, bonded block state, Jade icon, hammer orientation wheel, and adhesive endpoint preview all derive from the same synchronized surface data and share one cached result from Plasticraft's public procedural-texture API. Valid hammer and adhesive endpoint previews use the standard pale-blue plastic overlay; rejected previews use pale red. The world-aligned direction axes are centred on the model's actual rotation pivot. Inventory rendering normalises the longest visible surface-bounds axis to the same footprint as an ordinary universal plastic block and centres the actual surface; this does not change world size. A world entity samples block and sky light at the centre and six outer faces of its real bounds, so a small or offset model does not turn black on an opaque floor because its declared origin lies elsewhere. Hammer recovery, chunk reloads, and cross-dimensional travel do not reduce the product to the legacy block shape, and holding the item adds no placement preview.

A molded item's and entity's display name does not use the blueprint or model name. It combines the plastic material name with the final functional-type suffix: universal-plastic normal, chest, tank, anvil, and tray products are named "Universal Plastic Block", "Universal Plastic Chest", "Universal Plastic Tank", "Universal Plastic Anvil", and "Universal Plastic Tray". Future materials and functional types follow the same composition rule.

A molded entity has 24 discrete orientations: six attachment faces, each with four quarter-turns in its plane. Entity persistence and network synchronisation preserve both parts, while the saved manufacturing pivot keeps rendering, interaction outline, and physical collision aligned. Item placement follows the in-plane four-region rule above. Breaking, hammer-recovering, or pick-blocking an entity in any rotation returns the same model and material in the default item orientation.

Each complete or horizontally cut volumetric source cube becomes one continuous convex collision body. A dynamic entity uses those bodies directly, so a sloped cube keeps sloped physics and sloped F3+B edges instead of becoming many small boxes. A bonded block registers that same set of real convex bodies as SAT obstacles, preserving slopes for the final movement clipping of players, mobs, dropped items, and plastic entities. It also exposes a coarse `VoxelShape` made from one bounding box per body, but that shape is only for vanilla block APIs, broad-phase enumeration, and selection candidates; it neither overrides the real collision nor returns to per-pixel fitting. A zero-thickness cube renders on both sides and has a very thin interaction-only outline, so it can be selected and recovered but cannot support, block, or push an entity. Shapes up to the full 3x3x3 workspace retain the common plastic physics for gravity, buoyancy, dynamic attraction, pushing, sliding rails, magnetisation, adhesive bonding, persistence, and recovery.

While a piston builds its push list, the empty model-anchor cell occupied by each dynamic plastic entity presents its blockified state only for that structure resolution and consumes one push position. A piston facing a plastic entity therefore continues into and pushes the blocks in front of it, while an immovable block there correctly prevents extension. The placeholder returns to air as soon as resolution finishes, so it never settles as a block or duplicates the entity. A sticky piston retracts a plastic entity touching its head; a neighboring plastic entity joined to a slime block, honey block, or High-Viscosity Resin Block in the push structure also moves one block with that structure during both extension and retraction.

Entity movement, short support probes, stepping, carrying, and side pushing share these bodies and the same continuous SAT contact. Players, mobs, dropped items, and other supported entities follow one rule: movement over a slope stays on its real surface, and sneaking stops only at its real support edge. A plastic product supported by another entity receives its carried displacement during that supporter's current move instead of catching up on a later entity tick. Movement normal to and away from the product does not drag it along, but a gravity component in the same request does not cancel tangential carrying. Pushing a rotated cube transfers only the component aimed along the real contact normal, while the pusher retains movement along the face. If any product in a push chain is blocked, the pusher is clipped to the distance the whole chain can complete. Server-side player movement validation uses the same real convex bodies, so it does not mistake a compatibility bounding-box edge for a surface that can be stood on.

When a molded product's visible surface bounds are exactly `16 px` on X, Y, and Z, it receives and conducts redstone power through all six faces. Each input face first checks the cells touching its real surface and takes that layer's strongest directional signal, consulting the layer one cell farther out only when the near layer is zero; redstone wire contributes its current power level, and the strongest of all six faces is retained. Output follows the moving entity and projects from every face to the non-air, non-fluid-only receiver nearest that face centre across the near and far layers, so off-grid and bonded states use the same surface mapping as tray ports. The output is weak power rather than direct power; a receiver that conducts power continues to activate its neighbours by the normal vanilla rules. Virtual output from another full-sized plastic conductor is not accepted as input, preventing equal-strength conductors from latching after their external source is removed. The ordinary `16x16x14 px` universal plastic and every molded product with any axis other than `16 px` do not conduct.

An unspecified product, an explicitly normal product, and any future product downgraded to normal after partial forming all use the tooltip "An ordinary piece of plastic". Every universal plastic item also shows its exact three-axis size in blocks with at most four necessary decimal places. The ordinary `16x16x14 px` block displays `Size: 1 x 0.875 x 1 blocks`; a molded product uses its exact visible surface bounds and adds the six-face redstone-conduction line when all three axes are one block.

## Carry synchronisation and collision outlines

When a plastic product is genuinely supported by an entity's head or another face, it receives the tangential displacement during that carrier's current move. The client keeps ordered, unconfirmed movement segments per X, Y, and Z axis, so an immediate reversal does not cancel the two steps before the server confirms them. Snapshots consume the path in order, and a temporarily stationary snapshot does not discard that pending path; an upside-down hardened-resin cauldron therefore stays on a contacting rim or inner ceiling in the same tick and cannot be interpolated into the player's pose. Support, side pushing, and ordinary entities still use the same continuous convex bodies, while empty space inside the cauldron is not support.

With F3+B enabled, convex plastic collision is drawn as the real edge set of the union of every source cube. Shared faces, coplanar seams, and collinear segments are merged; concave openings, overlap boundaries, rotated cubes, and continuous slopes remain visible. The debug outline never falls back to a compatibility `VoxelShape` or one total bounding box; that compatibility shape remains limited to vanilla APIs and broad-phase queries. When the crosshair points at dynamic or bonded plastic, its selection outline reuses the same real edge set in black with an alpha of `0.4`; a dynamic outline follows the current interpolated render position and does not add a total bounding box.

## Plastic granules

Granules are deliberately limited to recipe or container processing. Each of these routes produces 16 granules with the melt's colour per `1000 mB`:

- the existing solid-liquid cooling recipe with a cold item;
- a Catalytic Press Lid outlet aimed at a full vanilla water cauldron.

These routes do not change the rule for a melt fluid block in the world. Each novice AnvilCraft Jeweler offer randomly requests one of the 16 colours, buying 8 granules of that colour for 2 emeralds, up to 16 trades per offer.
